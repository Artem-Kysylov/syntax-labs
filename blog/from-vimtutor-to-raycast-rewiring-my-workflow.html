From vimtutor to Raycast: How I Ditched the Mouse & Rewired My Workflow

By Artem Kysylov

Moving down the cybersecurity roadmap comes with one non-negotiable rule: you have to get comfortable inside a headless terminal without mice, GUIs, or glossy trackpads. And that naturally leads to every developer's favorite rite of passage: Vim.

Spoiler alert: Vim is definitely not something you master in a single relaxed evening. At first, your fingers tie themselves in literal knots, and your right hand involuntarily twitches toward the mouse every three seconds out of sheer panic. Here is how I survived the initial pain and turned keyboard-only navigation into a superpower.

1. The Survival Kit: vimtutor & Sticky Notes

I started right where everyone should: by opening the terminal and launching vimtutor. It’s a brilliant 30-minute interactive lesson that forces you to navigate using only HJKL, delete lines with dd, and figure out how the hell to exit the editor without turning off your monitor.

To bridge the gap between "what does this key do?" and actual productivity, I deployed my secret weapons: Google and a collection of bright yellow sticky notes pasted directly around my monitor frame.

"Learning Vim isn't like learning Python syntax—you don't study it conceptually. It is 100% pure muscle memory. Your brain doesn't think 'move left'; your index finger just twitches."

2. Bringing Vim Into VS Code & The Browser

Once the basics started sinking in, I didn't lock myself strictly in raw terminal sessions. I installed the official Vim extension directly inside VS Code.

The speed bump was instant. Executing commands like ciw (change inner word) or jumping straight to line definitions with keystrokes feels like pure magic once it clicks.

Naturally, I took it one step further and added Vimium to my browser. Suddenly, switching tabs, scrolling documentation, and clicking links happened purely via hotkeys. The trackpad was officially retired.

3. Beyond Editors: Controlling macOS with Raycast

Once you get hooked on home-row navigation, reaching for a mouse to open an app or search for a file feels ridiculously slow. To complete the setup, I configured Raycast across macOS.

Now window management, application launching, quick notes, and system commands all happen instantly with hotkeys. Everything just flies.

"Where the hell am I going to sit and cry? No keys, no mouse inside... SHIFT + :wq tonight!"
